Document 02 • Agreement

Website Development Agreement

Project specific agreement between Brad Hobbs Web Design and the client identified below.

Project specific terms: Attach or complete the approved proposal describing the scope, pricing, schedule, deliverables, and payment milestones. The proposal is incorporated into this agreement when both parties approve and sign.

1. Scope of Services

Brad Hobbs Web Design will provide the website design, development, and related services specifically listed in the approved proposal. Work outside the agreed scope requires written approval and may involve additional fees or schedule changes. No unlisted service, feature, page, integration, or content task is included by implication.

2. Fees & Payment

The client agrees to the project fee and payment schedule stated in the approved proposal. A required deposit must be received before project work begins. Remaining payments are due at the stated milestones or before final delivery, as described in the proposal. Late or unpaid balances may pause work. Third party charges, including domain registration, hosting, premium themes, plugins, subscriptions, payment processing, and other services, are the client's responsibility unless expressly included in writing.

3. Domain, Hosting & Accounts

The client purchases, owns, and maintains their domain name, hosting plan, and third party accounts. Required domain and hosting services must be purchased and activated before development begins. Brad Hobbs Web Design does not sell, provide, or own client domain or hosting accounts. The client will provide appropriate authorized access when required and is responsible for account fees, renewals, account security, and maintaining ownership. Credentials should be shared securely and never sent through an unsecured public channel.

4. Client Materials & Responsibilities

The client will provide accurate content, images, logos, product details, policies, credentials, approvals, and other materials needed for the project. The client represents that the client has permission to use supplied materials. The client is responsible for the accuracy, legality, accessibility, and rights associated with content and business claims, including privacy policies, terms, product claims, and regulatory requirements. Delays in providing materials or decisions may affect the schedule.

5. Design Review, Revisions & Approval

The proposal specifies the included review and revision rounds. Feedback should be consolidated and provided by the client's designated decision maker. Requests beyond included revisions, approved scope, or previously accepted design may be billed separately. Written approval of design, content, and launch readiness is required before final publication.

6. Timeline & Project Dependencies

Any timeline is an estimate based on timely client responses, payments, content delivery, account access, and third party availability. Brad Hobbs Web Design is not responsible for delays caused by missing client materials, client decisions, hosting providers, domain registrars, software vendors, payment services, or other outside dependencies. Significant changes may require a revised schedule.

7. E Commerce & Third Party Services

For online stores, the client is responsible for product accuracy, pricing, inventory, shipping, taxes, refund policies, customer service, legal compliance, payment provider approval, and transaction fees unless the proposal specifically assigns a task. Third party services may change their features, pricing, policies, availability, or technical requirements. No sales, search rankings, traffic, revenue, or business outcome is guaranteed.

8. Testing, Launch & Acceptance

The agreed project includes the testing described in the proposal. The client will review the website and report material issues before launch. Launch depends on client approval, required payments, active domain and hosting, and necessary account access. After launch, the client should maintain backups and account access. Additional post launch work is covered by the proposal or a separate service agreement.

9. Ownership & Portfolio

Upon payment in full, the client receives ownership of the final custom deliverables specifically identified in the proposal, subject to third party licenses, open source components, platform terms, and materials not owned by Brad Hobbs Web Design. Preexisting tools, reusable code, methods, templates, and know how remain the property of their respective owners. Unless the client requests otherwise in writing before launch, Brad Hobbs Web Design may display the completed project in its portfolio and identify the client for promotional purposes.

10. Ongoing Care & Support

Monthly website care, management, monitoring, updates, backups, or support are not included unless expressly selected in the proposal. Ongoing services, service limits, response expectations, fees, renewal, and cancellation terms should be documented separately or in the proposal. Third party platform and hosting maintenance may remain the client's responsibility.

11. Suspension, Cancellation & Termination

Either party may request termination in writing. The client remains responsible for payment for completed work, committed expenses, and approved work performed through the termination date, subject to the specific cancellation terms written in the proposal. Work may be paused for overdue payments, missing materials, unavailable access, or conduct that prevents completion. Any transfer of completed work or files will be subject to payment of outstanding amounts and the terms of the proposal.

12. Limitations & No Guaranteed Results

Brad Hobbs Web Design will perform services with reasonable care and professional effort. Website services cannot guarantee uninterrupted operation, security against every threat, compatibility with every device or browser, search placement, traffic, sales, or revenue. The parties should have an attorney review any limitation of liability, indemnification, warranty, or other risk allocation language added to this agreement.

13. Communications & Changes

Project approvals, scope changes, and material decisions should be documented in writing, including email. A change request may require revised fees, timeline, or terms and must be approved before additional work begins.

14. Governing Law & Entire Agreement

The governing law, dispute process, and any additional legal terms should be reviewed and completed by qualified legal counsel for the parties' circumstances. This agreement and its approved proposal represent the project terms and supersede prior project discussions to the extent stated by applicable law. Changes must be in writing and accepted by both parties.

15. Signatures

By signing, the parties confirm that they have read and understand the agreement and the approved proposal incorporated into the agreement.
